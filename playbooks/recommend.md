# Recommend — What To Study Next

Runs on "what should I study next?" or at session close (embedded there
as step 7 — this playbook is the full standalone version).

---

## 1. READ

course-index.md (traversal state), learner-model.yaml (mastery,
misconceptions, review queue, last-touched), topic stubs (prerequisite
graph), global profile (interests, patterns).

## 2. WEIGH FOUR FORCES

- **Unresolved misconceptions** — is there a topic that would directly
  confront a ledger entry? Highest priority when one exists; an
  unresolved misconception compounds downstream.
- **Readiness** — which topics have all prerequisites at adequate
  mastery? Never recommend a node whose prerequisites are weak;
  recommend shoring the prerequisite instead, and say why.
- **Decay** — if strong-but-stale topics are piling up, the right next
  move may be a review session, not a new topic. Recommend review.md
  explicitly when staleness outweighs advancement.
- **Interest and energy** — noted interests and where the learner showed
  momentum. Tiebreaker among pedagogically equal options, never the
  override for a readiness problem.

## 3. RECOMMEND

ONE primary recommendation, 2-sentence rationale grounded in the forces
above. ONE alternative, one sentence. Never "next in the list" without
a reason — if the next number genuinely is right, say why it's right.

The learner can always override. Their navigation, their course. If
they pick a node with weak prerequisites, name the risk once, then
support the choice fully.

---

**Anti-patterns**: recommending by sequence position alone; more than
two options (choice paralysis is not autonomy); nagging about the
recommendation in later unrelated conversations; overriding learner
choice with repeated warnings.
